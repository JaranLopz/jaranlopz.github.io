Chapter 02 • Common Pitch Red Flags & Solutions
The Due Diligence Gap

3 Red Flags That Make Publishers Pass on Good Prototypes

A playable Unreal or Unity demo and organic YouTube views will get you an introductory call. But before a publisher wires $1M–$5M, their production and finance teams run due diligence. Click on any red flag below to see how we solve it:

Red Flag #1

Unproven Scope & Asset Math

You know what game you want to build, but you can’t prove how 12 developers will deliver 300+ unique 3D assets, complex AI, and 60 FPS optimization in 30 months without running out of cash.

🔧 How I help you fix this ▼

The Solution: We break down your asset list into modular pipelines (e.g., separating base meshes from historical or cosmetic variations) and defer high-risk engineering features (like full Campaign AI or multiplayer) out of your pre-funding build so you can ship a rock-solid 30-minute Vertical Slice first.

Recommended Service: Pitch Readiness Audit →
Red Flag #2

Broken Financial & Break-Even Math

Your budget spreadsheet is a rough estimate. Publishers immediately spot wrong tax formulas, unrealistic recoup timelines, and sales targets that aren't backed by real Steam genre data.

🔧 How I help you fix this ▼

The Solution: I audit your entire P&L spreadsheet formula by formula—fixing how Steam cuts, VAT, publisher recoup waterfalls, and corporate taxes interact. Then we benchmark your break-even unit count against real SteamDB/Boxleiter genre comps so investors see a credible 3x–6x ROI path.

Recommended Service: €250 Red-Flag Review →
Red Flag #3

No Senior Producer in the Room

Publishers rarely wire $1M–$5M to talented engineers and artists who have never managed a multi-year production cycle or a 15-person hiring ramp-up. They want someone protecting the schedule.

🔧 How I help you fix this ▼

The Solution: We build a 15–20 page Publisher Pre-Read Document (covering your 36-month Gantt roadmap, hiring ramp-up, and risk register), and I can join your pitch roster as your Fractional Executive Producer—co-signing milestones and running weekly leadership reviews.

Chapter 04 • Detailed Client Case Study
CASE STUDY • UNREAL ENGINE 5 GRAND STRATEGY GAME • $5M / 36-MONTH PITCH

Preparing a $5M Publisher Pitch & Fixing a $500K Budget Error for a 6-Person Strategy Studio

Client: Broken Rampart Interactive (an independent European team of 6 part-time developers, including a former NVIDIA GPU optimization architect). Project: Project Renaissance, a historical medieval grand strategy and real-time battle game set between 1066 and 1550.

01 / The Starting Point

Strong Tech & Organic Traction, Missing Studio Structure

Before we started working together, the team had solved the hardest technical problem of their genre: they built a custom Unreal Engine 5 battle prototype capable of rendering 10,000 to 40,000 soldiers on screen at 60–80 FPS, alongside a procedural city generator for medieval London.

Organic coverage from strategy YouTubers had already generated over 600,000 YouTube views and a 1,300+ member Discord community, which caught the attention of tier-1 strategy publishers (including Hooded Horse). However, the founder was a technical CEO with no prior game production background. To justify a $5,060,000 USD budget across 36 months (scaling from 6 part-timers to 15–19 full-time developers), they needed a credible production plan, a de-risked asset pipeline, and a financial model that could survive publisher due diligence.

02 / What We Did Together

De-Risking the Vertical Slice & Structuring the Diligence Bible

I stepped in as Consulting Producer to audit their scope, pipeline, and documentation before publisher submission:

  • Protecting the 30-Minute Vertical Slice: The team had not yet started building Campaign AI or 8-player multiplayer—two massive engineering sinks. Instead of letting those stall their pre-funding build, we locked the immediate scope around a tight, scripted 30-minute playable Vertical Slice loop and moved full Campaign AI and multiplayer into gated, funded Alpha milestones.
  • Modular 3D & Animation Pipeline: Their historian and art lead scoped 400 historical armors (300 unique) and 800 medieval buildings. To make that believable on an indie headcount, we structured a modular gear pipeline, motion-capture suit workflows, and clear outsourcer budgets.
  • 3-Part Publisher Pitch Package: Instead of sending a single bloated slide deck, we separated the pitch into a short Cold Email Teaser, a 15-slide visual Sales Deck for live meetings, and a comprehensive 20-page Publisher Pre-Read Diligence Document containing the 36-month Gantt roadmap, hiring ramp-up, and risk register.
03 / The Concrete Numbers

Unlocking +$500K (+76%) in Studio Profit & Dropping Break-Even by 20%

When auditing the studio’s budget projection spreadsheet ($40 SRP • 50/50 publisher revenue split), I found a critical formula error in their recoup waterfall: the spreadsheet was deducting a 20% corporate tax rate directly from gross post-Steam revenue ($8.0M at 400,000 units) before subtracting the $5.06M development investment.

Because corporate tax only applies to net profit after deductible development costs are recouped, fixing the financial model completely changed the pitch’s viability:

  • Break-Even Threshold Lowered by 20%: Under the broken formula, the game needed 316,250 copies just to recoup the $5.06M budget. Correcting the tax and recoup logic dropped the real break-even point down to 253,000 units (63,250 fewer copies required to break even).
  • Projected Studio Net Profit Increased by +76% (+$507K): At their target scenario of 400,000 copies sold ($8.0M post-Steam revenue), the studio’s 50% net profit share rose from an undercounted $669,000 USD up to $1,176,000 USD.
  • Real Corporate Market Comps: To validate the commercial ceiling for investors, we combined SteamDB Boxleiter multipliers with official UK Companies House financial filings for The Creative Assembly / SEGA (£112.28M annual turnover / £98.13M dev spend)—demonstrating how a focused $5M Unreal Engine 5 title can capture high-margin demand in the medieval strategy gap.
